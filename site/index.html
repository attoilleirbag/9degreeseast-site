    Across low-carbon fuels

    eFuels

    Fuels made from green hydrogen and captured CO2

    Synthetic fuels such as e-methanol, e-gasoline and e-SAF, produced with renewable power. Projects are capital-intensive, so long-term offtake that reflects hydrogen, CO2 and power costs is what gets them financed.

    Discuss an eFuels project →

    Green methanol

    A leading fuel for the new generation of ships

    Methanol made from biomass or from green hydrogen and CO2. Shipping lines are ordering dual-fuel vessels to meet FuelEU Maritime, and the same molecule serves the chemicals market, so supply and pricing structures have to work across both.

    Discuss a methanol project →

    SAF

    Sustainable aviation fuel

    Jet fuel made from waste oils, residues or synthetic routes, blended with conventional kerosene. ReFuelEU Aviation sets rising blending mandates in Europe, and US incentives support production, so airlines and producers need supply locked in well ahead of demand.

    Discuss a SAF project →

    Renewable diesel

    A drop-in replacement for fossil diesel

    Also known as HVO: diesel made by hydrotreating vegetable oils and waste fats, chemically equivalent to fossil diesel and usable without blending limits. Value depends heavily on feedstock and on regulation such as RED in the EU and the RFS and LCFS in the US.

    Discuss a renewable diesel project →

    Renewable natural gas

    Biomethane for pipelines, transport and industry

    Biogas from landfills, farms and wastewater, upgraded to pipeline quality. Interchangeable with natural gas, its value is driven largely by environmental credits, which makes contract structure and credit allocation central to every deal.
