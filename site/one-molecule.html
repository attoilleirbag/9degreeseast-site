01 · Wind

It starts as a gust.

A turbine on a windy coast turns moving air into electricity. That power is the real raw material: everything that follows runs on it.

Making e-fuels takes a lot of electricity. The power bill is one of the biggest numbers in any project’s model.

02 · Water

Water is split in two.

In an electrolyser, that electricity pulls water apart into oxygen and hydrogen. The hydrogen is what our molecule is built on.

03 · Carbon

Carbon is caught, not dug up.

The carbon comes from CO₂ captured from biogenic sources or straight from the air, CO₂ that would otherwise be lost to the atmosphere.

Where the CO₂ comes from matters: it decides whether the fuel counts as renewable under the rules.

05 · Proof

Green has to be proved, not claimed.

Before the molecule counts towards any target, an auditor checks where every input came from. In the EU, the hydrogen must run on new renewable power, matched to production in time and place.

Time matching is monthly until the end of 2029, then hourly from 2030. The finished fuel must also cut greenhouse gas emissions by at least 70% compared with fossil fuel.

07 · The gap

Cleaner, but several times the price.

Today, fuel made this way costs several times as much as the fossil fuel it replaces. Mandates, penalties and carbon prices are what close the gap, for now.

The gap narrows as electrolysers and renewable power get cheaper. Our Fuel Weather tracks the power price signal every week.

08 · The part nobody sees

None of it gets built without a signature.

This is the offtake agreement. It’s the quiet document that turns a project on paper into a project in the ground.

Every molecule starts with a deal.

The wind, the water and the chemistry are the visible part. The agreements that make lenders say yes are the part that decides whether any of it gets built.
