What is an offtake agreement, and why does it matter so much?

An offtake agreement is a contract in which a buyer commits to buy a share of a plant’s future output, at an agreed price or formula, for an agreed number of years. For a new low-carbon fuel plant it is usually the document lenders care about most, because it shows the plant’s revenue before it is built. Without it, most projects cannot raise debt.

What makes a deal “bankable”?

Broadly: enough of the output is contracted, for long enough, with a creditworthy buyer, at prices that cover costs and debt service with room to spare, and with risks such as volume shortfalls, regulation changes and certification clearly allocated. The exact tests depend on the lenders and the project. Our How we work page has examples of deals that closed and one that stalled.
