Deal structuring

Price, volume, credit and sustainability terms that work for both sides.

01

What it is

Deal structuring turns an agreed commercial intent into terms that the producer, the buyer and their lenders can all sign.

In low-carbon fuels the difficult parts are the same on almost every deal. There is no liquid forward market for green methanol or e-SAF to price against. New plants carry ramp-up risk. The seller is often a new project company while the buyer wants flexibility. And the value of the fuel depends on certification that has to hold for the life of the contract.

  • Price. Fixed with indexation, cost pass-through for hydrogen, power, CO2 or feedstock, or a fossil index plus a premium, often with a floor and a cap.
  • Volume. Take-or-pay or take-and-pay, ramp-up schedules, tolerance bands, make-up and carry-forward rights.
  • Credit. Parent guarantees, letters of credit, prepayment and step-in rights.
  • Sustainability. Certification scheme (ISCC EU, RSB and others), carbon intensity thresholds, mass balance or book-and-claim, and who owns which certificates.
  • Risk allocation. Change in law, force majeure, construction delay and termination payments.
02

When projects need it

  • Once a buyer reaches term sheet stage and the headline price has to become a formula that survives ten years of market moves.
  • When the two sides want different things, for example lenders asking for minimum revenue while the buyer will only pay for compliant volumes.
  • When public support has to fit around the contract. The UK's revenue certainty contracts for SAF, set up under the Sustainable Aviation Fuel Act 2026, pay producers the difference to a strike price. The EU Hydrogen Bank pays a fixed premium. ETS allowances set aside for SAF cover part of the price gap. Each changes what the offtake contract needs to carry.
  • When an existing agreement no longer works because costs, rules or volumes have moved.

How we help

  • Model the deal from both sides, drawing on Tom's time as both a developer and a buyer: the producer's debt service against what the fuel is worth to the buyer. That value depends on where the fuel counts across overlapping regulations, and on its production pathway, since e-fuels carry sub-mandates and multipliers that biofuels do not.
  • Design and stress-test the price formula against power, feedstock, fossil fuel and credit price scenarios.
  • Write the term sheet and negotiate it alongside the client's lawyers, with lenders' requirements built in from the start.
