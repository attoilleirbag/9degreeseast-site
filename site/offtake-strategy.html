Offtake strategy

Who to sell to, on what terms, and how to sequence it for financing.

01

What it is

Without buyers, a fuels project is an expensive PowerPoint. Offtake strategy decides who buys the plant's output, how much of it, for how long and on what pricing basis, before the first contract is signed.

For a new fuels project it is the commercial case lenders read first. Debt is sized against contracted revenue, so the shape of the offtake book sets how much the project can borrow and on what terms.

  • Buyer segments. Fuel suppliers and airlines with obligations under ReFuelEU Aviation or the UK SAF Mandate. Shipping lines facing FuelEU Maritime and the EU ETS. Chemical buyers for methanol. Road fuel blenders under RED III, the RFS and California's LCFS.
  • Contracted share. How much capacity to sell long term, how much to hold back for spot sales or later contracts, and in what order to sign.
  • Attributes. Whether certificates, RINs, LCFS credits or mandate tickets travel with the fuel or are sold separately, and to whom.
02

When projects need it

Early, ideally before front-end engineering is finished. Lenders generally want most of a plant's capacity under bankable contracts before financial close, and a plan made after the design is fixed leaves fewer options on site, product slate and logistics.

  • Before an equity raise or a sale process, when investors want to see who will buy the fuel.
  • When the rules move demand. The US 45Z credit lost its extra SAF incentive from 2026 and now only rewards North American feedstocks. Global shipping rules are still unsettled: the IMO postponed its Net-Zero Framework in October 2025, so for now EU regulation drives most firm demand for green marine fuel.
  • When an anchor buyer walks away and the book has to be rebuilt without losing the financing timetable.
03

How we help

  • Map the buyers by compliance need, credit quality and appetite for long tenors, using the obligations each segment actually faces.
  • Build in flexibility. Contract and certify so the fuel can serve more than one market as rules and prices change: for example, methanol across shipping and chemicals, HEFA output across road and aviation, and biomethane across grid and transport.
  • Size and sequence the book: an anchor buyer first, then follow-on contracts, timed around the financing.
  • Set the pricing basis for each segment: fixed with indexation, cost-plus, or a fossil benchmark plus a green premium, with floors and caps where lenders need them.
  • Run the outreach and term sheets, drawing on two decades of origination across biofuels, eFuels and green methanol.
